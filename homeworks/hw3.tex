\paragraph{Problem 1 (20 points)}
A function $f: \mathbb{R}^n\rightarrow \mathbb{R}$ is convex if for any $x \in \real^n, y\in \real^n$, and $\alpha \in (0, 1)$, $\alpha f(x) + (1 - \alpha) f(y) \ge f( \alpha x + (1 - \alpha) y )$.

Let $f$ be a convex function taking as input a vector $x\in \mathbb{R}^n$.
Assume that $f$ is twice-differentiable.
The Hessian matrix of $f$, denoted by $\mathbf{H} = \nabla^2 f$, is a square matrix in $\real^{n\times n}$ with the $(i,j)$-th entry of defined as:
\[ \mathbf{H}[f]_{i,j} = \frac{\partial^2 f(x)}{\partial x_i \partial x_j}. \]
\begin{itemize}
    \item (5 points) Suppose that $n = 2$, and $f(x) = x_1^2 + x_1 x_2 + x_2^2$. Show that $f(x)$ is a convex function.

    Can you give an example of a nonconvex function and explain why it is nonconvex?

    \item (5 points) A function $f$ is $L$-smooth if $\bignorm{\nabla f(x) - \nabla f(y)} \le L \bignorm{x - y}$. Is $f(x) = x_1^2 + x_1 x_2 + x_2^2$ smooth? What is the $L$-smoothness parameter of this particular function?

    \item (5 points) Given a convex function with $n = 1$, can you show that its Hessian matrix $\mathbf{H}[f]$ is positive semidefinite, meaning that $v^{\top} \mathbf{H}[f] v \ge 0$, for any $v \in \real$?

    [Hint: Convexity implies that the first-derivative is monotone, hence the second-derivative is non-negative.]

    \item (5 points) Can you extend the above to the case where $n$ is in arbitrary dimension?
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Problem 2 (45 points).}
Suppose that $\cD$ is an unknown distribution supported in the $d$-dimensional space $\mathbb{R}^d$.
Recall that the covariance matrix of a distribution $\cD$ is defined as
\[ \Sigma = \exarg{x\sim \cD}{(x - \exarg{x\sim \cD}{x})(x-\exarg{x\sim p^{\star}}{x})^\top }. \]
\begin{itemize}
    \item (5 points) Given $n$ independent samples from $\cD$, $v_1, v_2, \dots, v_n$ from $\real^d$. Define the sample covariance matrix
    \[ \widehat{\Sigma} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i = 1}^n v_i v_i^{\top}. \]
    What is the expectation of $\widehat \Sigma$?
    
    \item (5 points) Suppose that every $x_i \in \real$ is sampled independently from the Gaussian distribution $\cN(0, 1)$ with mean zero and variance one. What is the covariance matrix of the vector $x = [x_1, x_2, \dots, x_d]^{\top} \in \real^d$?
   
    \item (15 points) Next, suppose that the distribution $\cD$ is centered at zero, and for any $i = 1,2,\dots,n$, the largest coordinate of $v_i$ are bounded by $B$: $||v_i||_{\infty}\leq B$.
    For any fixed $\epsilon > 0$, show that
    \[ \mathbb{P}\Big{[}\max_{1\leq i,j\leq d}\bigabs{\widehat{\Sigma}_{i, j} - \Sigma_{i, j}}\geq \epsilon\Big{]}\leq 2d^2\exp \left(-\frac{n\epsilon^2}{2B^4}\right). \]

    \item (10 points) Lastly, in the case of $n$ sampled Gaussian vectors $v_1, v_2, \dots, v_n$ from $\real^d$ with mean zero and isotropic covariance matrix $\id_d$, show that $\bignorm{v_i}_\infty \le \sqrt{2 \log\Big(\frac{2nd}{\delta} \Big)}$ for every $i = 1, 2, \dots, n$ with probability at least $1 - \delta$.

    \item (10 points) Conduct a python numerical experiment to verify the above result. That is, draw a figure to compare $\bignormFro{\widehat \Sigma - \Sigma}$ as a function of $n$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Problem 4 (10 points).}
In this problem, you will work through an example to compute the stationary policy and the optimal value function of a Markov decision process.

Consider a discounted infinite-horizon MDP with discount factor $\gamma = 0.8$.
The state space is $\cS = {s_1, s_2, s_3}$ and the action space is $\cA = {a_1, a_2}$.
The transition model (which is deterministic in this case) and the reward function are given as follows.
\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{c|c|c|c}
\toprule
State & Action & Next state & Reward \\
\hline
$s_1$ & $a_1$ & $s_1$ & 2 \\
$s_1$ & $a_2$ & $s_2$  & 0 \\
$s_2$ & $a_1$ & $s_3$ & 3 \\
$s_2$ & $a_2$ & $s_1$  & 1 \\
$s_3$ & $a_1$ & $s_3$  & 1 \\
$s_3$ & $a_2$ & $s_2$ & 0 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\begin{itemize}
    \item (10 points) Consider the stationary policy $\pi$ defined by $\pi(s_1)=a_2, \pi(s_2)=a_1,\pi(s_3)=a_1.$
    Write down the linear system of equations satisfied by the value function $V^{\pi}$.
    Then, solve the linear system and compute the value function $V^\pi$ corresponding to $\pi$.
    
    [Hint: Note that the policies here are deterministic. Refer to Section 5 of the lecture notes.]
    
    \item (10 points bonus) Starting from $V_0(s)=0$ for all $s \in \cS$, perform $3$ steps of value iteration for $k = 0, 1, 2,$
    \[ V_{k+1}(s)=\max_{a\in\cA}\left(r(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s'\in\cS}\Pr[s'|s,a] V_k(s')\right), \text{ for every } s \in \cS, \]
    and obtain $V_1, V_2, V_3$. Then, use $V_3$ to compute the greedy policy:
    \[ \pi(s) = \arg\max_{a\in\cA}\left(r(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s'\in\cS} \Pr[s'|s,a] V_3(s')\right), \text{ for every } s \in \cS. \]
\end{itemize}
